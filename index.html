Ignacio Verdugo

PhD student in Medical Radiation Physics, Stockholm University

I'm a medical physicist and PhD student at Stockholm University, and an affiliated graduate researcher at Karolinska Institutet. My research focuses on mathematical modelling of how tumours and the immune system respond to radiotherapy combined with immunotherapy.

Before my PhD, I spent six years at Elekta as a clinical application specialist and master trainer for the Monaco treatment planning system and MOSAIQ, training clinical teams in hospitals across Latin America and Spain in Spanish, English and Portuguese.

Research

Current work

My PhD project models tumour and immune response to radiotherapy combined with immunotherapy, fitting mathematical models to pre-clinical data. I work with the Medical Radiation Physics group at Stockholm University and Karolinska Institutet.

Earlier projects

  • 2017 – 2019

    Quantifying radiodermatitis with image processing

    Master's thesis, University of São Paulo

    Used colour imaging and image processing to estimate the grade of radiodermatitis in breast cancer patients undergoing photon radiotherapy.

  • 2016

    Monte Carlo simulation of a 6 MV linac

    Undergraduate thesis, Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile

    Modelled a 6 MV linear accelerator with the EGSnrc Monte Carlo code.

  • 2015

    Dose distributions for small-animal irradiation

    Research assistant, Queensland University of Technology

    Studied photon distribution, scattering and absorption in an 8 × 8 × 5 cm water phantom of a mouse with air gaps and lead shielding, to find the most suitable energies and lead widths for different treatments. Funded by QUT's International Research Experience Scholarship.

  • 2013

    Sensitivity of TLD-100 dosimeters

    Research assistant, Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile

    Followed the response of a new batch of 100 TLD-100 dosimeters through six successive irradiations in a 6 MV photon beam.
